01

Start with the reading flow

A sticky element remains in normal document flow until the scroll position reaches its offset. After that point, it holds its position while its containing block still has room for it.

This makes sticky positioning useful for table of contents links, labels, and navigation that should remain available without covering the entire page.

02

Keep the context intact

The sticky navigation belongs inside the same layout as the article. As the article grows, the navigation can remain visible, but it will stop at the bottom of the layout instead of escaping its containing block.

The offset is important. Without an inset such as inset block start, the browser has no distance from the top edge at which to hold the element.

03

Test the overflow trap

Sticky positioning can silently fail when an ancestor creates an unexpected scrolling container. This is easy to cause with overflow: hidden, even when the wrapper was only meant to clip a visual effect.

In this demo, the wrapper is intentionally left visible so the page itself remains the scroll container.

04

Finish with a useful pattern

Sticky positioning is a small feature, but it improves long pages because readers can keep their place without scrolling back to the top. The important part is checking the offset and the overflow behavior of every ancestor.
